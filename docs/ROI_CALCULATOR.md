# ROI calculator — what running an escrow on AE402 actually costs

For a potential integrator asking "what does this cost me per
transaction, and what am I paying for". Every gas figure below is a real
measured number from `docs/GAS_BENCHMARK.md` (sourced from actual
confirmed testnet deploys, not estimates) — this file adds the pricing
layer on top, it introduces no new gas claims.

## 1. Per-transaction cost (testnet gas, in CSPR)

| Lifecycle step | Gas consumed (CSPR) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `create` | 3.625 | Fund a new escrow |
| `release` | 3.176 | Happy-path payout to receiver |
| `refund` | 2.970 | Sender reclaims funds (TTL expiry or agreement) |
| `dispute` | 0.648 | Flag a disagreement (no fund movement) |
| `resolve` (3-of-5 arbiter multisig) | 7.537 | Arbiter-adjudicated payout after dispute |

**Happy path (create → release):** 3.625 + 3.176 = **6.8 CSPR gas**, plus
the platform's insurance fee (below).

**Disputed path (create → dispute → resolve):** 3.625 + 0.648 + 7.537 =
**11.8 CSPR gas** — roughly 1.7x the happy path, entirely attributable to
the cost of on-chain 3-of-5 signature verification, which is the price of
not trusting a single arbitrator.

To convert to fiat: multiply by the current CSPR/USD rate. This document
intentionally does not hardcode a CSPR price — check
<https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/casper-network> for the current rate
and multiply; a hardcoded number here would go stale immediately.

## 2. Platform fee

AE402 takes an **insurance fee** on top of gas, configurable per
deployment (`INSURANCE_FEE_BPS`, default **200 bps = 2%**,
`server/config.py`). This funds the insurance pool that pays out on
verified service-failure claims (see `docs/evidence/` for the pool
contract). It is deducted from the escrowed amount at `create` time — see
`_apply_insurance_fee` in `server/app.py`.

**Example, 1,000 CSPR escrow, happy path:**
- Insurance fee: 1,000 × 2% = 20 CSPR (receiver gets net 980 CSPR)
- Gas: ~6.8 CSPR (paid separately by whoever submits each deploy)
- Total cost of using escrow instead of a direct transfer: **~26.8 CSPR
  (~2.7% of the escrowed amount)** for the guarantee that funds only move
  on agreed terms, with dispute resolution and insurance-backed recourse
  available if something goes wrong.

## 3. What you're paying for, vs. a direct transfer

A direct peer-to-peer CSPR transfer costs a flat, tiny gas fee and gives
you *no* guarantees beyond "the transfer happened". AE402's ~2.7%
all-in cost on a 1,000 CSPR escrow buys:

- **Conditional release** — funds move only when the agreed condition
  (explicit release call, or arbiter resolution) is met, not unilaterally.
- **Dispute path with real recourse** — a disagreement doesn't mean "funds
  are just gone to whoever grabbed them first"; it routes through
  VRF-selected, staked arbiters (`docs/evidence/VRF_ONCHAIN_ELECTION.md`).
- **Insurance-backed payout** on a verified claim, funded by the pooled
  fee, not by the counterparty who may be unable or unwilling to pay.
- **Optional amount privacy** (Pedersen commitment + range proof,
  `docs/ZK_AMOUNT_PRIVACY.md`) at no extra gas cost over a standard
  escrow, if `confidential: true` is set.

## 4. Break-even framing for an integrator

If you're building an agent-to-agent payments product and weighing
"roll my own escrow logic" vs. "integrate AE402":

- **Engineering cost saved**: the FSM, dispute/arbitration flow, insurance
  pool, and multi-asset support here represent the several-week build
  documented across `docs/tier3/` and the Tier 1–3 backlog — not a
  weekend script.
- **Ongoing cost you take on**: the ~2.7% fee + gas above, per escrow, is
  the ceiling — the insurance fee is configurable per deployment if 2% is
  higher than your margin tolerates (see `INSURANCE_FEE_BPS`).
- **Cost of getting it wrong**: an unaudited home-built escrow that
  mishandles the fund-then-release race (see `server/flash_guard.py` /
  T2.12) or replay-attacks the insurance claim path (see
  `docs/INSURANCE_REPLAY_TESTS.md`) is a security incident, not a line
  item — these are exactly the classes of bug this project has already
  found and fixed once, so an integrator doesn't have to re-discover them.

## Caveats

- Gas figures are testnet measurements; mainnet gas *pricing* (not gas
  *consumption* — Wasm execution cost is deterministic regardless of
  network) should be roughly equivalent since Casper's gas-per-op cost
  table doesn't change between testnet and mainnet, but this hasn't been
  independently re-verified on mainnet by this project.
- The 369 testnet transactions behind these numbers were generated by the
  project's own funded test wallets exercising the full test suite and
  evidence-collection scripts — see the usage-metrics disclaimer in
  `TX_MANIFEST.md`. They are real on-chain executions, not synthetic
  benchmarks, but they are not third-party production traffic.
